#### The problem
Business-register records do not always show which shops, services and other businesses are operating on the ground. Local economy officers spend time comparing records with websites, maps and street observations. They need a useful overview with evidence they can check.
This challenge draws on interviews with 10 local economy officers about their everyday work. Local economy officers will be available as mentors and are also represented on the jury. Bring them focused questions about identifying active businesses, checking evidence and finding the right contact to validate your assumptions and improve your prototype.
#### What to build
Build a tool for officers that brings business information and public evidence together. Show what is known, what may have changed and what remains uncertain. Distinguish and connect local establishments, their enterprise record and registered seat, which may be elsewhere. Let the officer inspect the evidence, correct records and approve proposed changes before publication.
Finding the right business contact is another useful feature: show available contact details, whether they belong to the local establishment or central office, and their source. Contact information may be based outside the municipality. No specific contact fields are mandatory; make missing or uncertain details visible.
#### Three success criteria ✅
1. **Reliable business data** — find missing or inaccurate records, flag potentially inactive entries, and enrich the data with evidence and confidence.
2. **Useful and trustworthy for officers** — make records easy to find, inspect and correct, with officer approval before publication.
3. **Fresh and reusable** — show how the data stays up to date and how the approach adapts to another municipality or province.
The jury judges this challenge on the three success criteria above.

## Source 3: 🔎 Examples & Technical Guidance

Canonical page: [🔎 Examples & Technical Guidance](https://app.notion.com/p/3d5d61b47ae281df9707dd2c9063b056)

**Use what helps your team.** These examples, walkthroughs and design ideas support the three success criteria on the challenge page. They do not add requirements or scoring items. Choose your own municipality or region inside the Province of Antwerp. You are free to choose your own data sources and tools. The Schoten KBO sample is optional.

#### Officer context
Municipalities lack a reliable view of the businesses actually active on their territory. The federal business register — the **KBO** (*Kruispuntbank van Ondernemingen*) — lists companies that stopped trading long ago as active, misses shops that are visibly open, and buries the local economy under dormant registrations and side activities. One municipality of about 20,000 residents counted roughly 33,000 register entries.
> *"One of our biggest problems is simply knowing which businesses are actually active in our municipality."* — a local economy officer in the province
Officers fill the gaps with street visits, Google searches and paid data brokers.
- **Marleen** cross-checks records by hand, in Excel, between emails. Outreach can miss new shops or reach businesses that have closed.
- **Tom** pays a commercial data provider to fill the gap and pulls the results into his own database. He wants to compare public sources and see how the tool reached its conclusion.
#### Enterprise or establishment?

**Enterprise and establishment: two different records**
An **enterprise** (*onderneming*) is the legal entity: a company, an association or a sole trader with a registry number. An **establishment unit** (*vestigingseenheid*) is a physical place where that enterprise operates: a shop, an office, a workshop. One enterprise can have several establishments, in several municipalities, and its registered seat may be somewhere else entirely.
Distinguish between a business's local establishments and its enterprise record and registered seat. Both matter. Establishments help officers understand activity on the ground; enterprise information helps identify the organisation behind them and the appropriate contact, which may be based elsewhere. Keep these records connected and make their relationship clear (in the starter data: column `Ondernemingsnr_maatsch_zetel`). Legal status, bankruptcy and dissolution are recorded on the enterprise, not on the establishment.

#### Optional worked example

**"Welke ondernemingen zijn actief in deze straat, en waarop baseren we dat?"**

*Which businesses are active in this street, and what do we base that on?*

Show each **address with a status, dated evidence and an uncertainty marker**. This fictional example uses the officer's language:

| Adres | Onderneming / vestiging | Register | Bewijs van activiteit | Laatste waarneming | Zekerheid | Voorstel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Voorbeeldstraat 12 | Bakkerij Voorbeeld (vestiging) | Actief | Website actief; openingsuren op kaartdienst bevestigd | 2026-09-05 | Hoog | Geen actie |
| Voorbeeldstraat 14 | Adviesbureau Voorbeeld (vestiging, zetel elders) | Actief | Geen website, geen vermelding, geen recente vergunning | — | Laag | Ter controle: mogelijk niet meer actief |
| Voorbeeldstraat 20 | Kapsalon Voorbeeld (niet in register op dit adres) | — | Uithangbord en recensies vermelden dit adres | 2026-09-05 | Middel | Nazicht: vestiging ontbreekt of adres verkeerd |

The officer ticks *bevestigen* or *afwijzen* per row; only confirmed changes leave the tool.
**Optional contact view for this fictional example:** When the officer opens *Bakkerij Voorbeeld*, the tool could show any available phone number or email, whether it belongs to the local establishment or central office, a clickable source and when it was checked. If the contact is at a central office elsewhere, show how it connects to the local establishment and enterprise. If no contact is known, display *contactgegevens onbekend* rather than inventing details. Finding the right contact is a useful feature; no specific contact fields are mandatory.
#### Optional starter materials
The shared [📁 Starter Files & Source Guide](https://app.notion.com/p/ab0b79e26700467c82c645f773536c24) contains the ZIP, complete inventory, field descriptions, provenance, licence notes and known gaps. Its KBO folder has an optional sample of 1,000 Schoten records; you may use, adapt or replace it.
If you follow the walkthrough below, read the sample's notes first: it mixes legal entities and establishments, is incomplete, has sparse activity fields and some suspect coordinates, and does not state the exact underlying federal snapshot date. Other maps, listings, permit records or websites may need their own access; no external API keys are supplied.
#### A suggested first 35 minutes
*A suggestion to get moving — not a minimum requirement and not a scoring threshold.*
1. **0–10 min · Load and look.** Import the CSV with **registry numbers as text** (they have leading zeros). Split the rows into enterprises and establishments. Note which columns are actually filled.
2. **10–20 min · Pick one street.** Choose a street with a good number of records (in the sample, Paalstraat has the most, 35). List its records, group them by address, and mark what you can and cannot tell from the register alone.
3. **20–35 min · Compare with one public source.** Take five to ten records and check them against a source you can access: a map service, business listings, the business's own website. Write down for each: source, what you observed, observation date.

#### Officer context
A local economy officer may handle questions about terrace permits, market stalls, opening a shop, signage and inspection letters. Relevant sources include **federal** legislation, **Flemish** decrees and guidance, **provincial** regulations, **municipal** bylaws and previous replies in a shared mailbox.
> *"That knowledge is in my head."* — a local economy officer in the province
A typical reply takes about forty minutes of searching across tabs and PDFs. When an officer is away or leaves the role, colleagues may struggle to find the same information. Interviews also highlighted concern about AI-generated errors: officers need to inspect the supporting passages, check whether the source applies to their municipality and question, and identify outdated or missing information.
- **Marleen** answers from memory and old emails. She wants the relevant rule quoted so she can check it before replying.
- **Tom** wants to manage the document collection himself, see exactly which passage produced which sentence, and hand the same setup to a neighbouring municipality.
#### Optional example · A market application

#### Optional first 35 minutes · A retrieval-based prototype 💡
*A suggestion to get moving — not a minimum requirement and not a scoring threshold.*
1. **0–10 min · Select and describe.** Choose documents relevant to your chosen question; the market regulation and fee regulation are one example. For each, record: title, issuing authority, legal level, date or "undated", status (in force / guidance / historical), page count.
2. **10–20 min · Extract with metadata.** Pull the text page by page and keep **page numbers, chapter and article numbers** attached to every chunk. For the market example, compare page 5 of the original PDF with your extraction: tables, footnotes and paragraph marks (§) break easily.
3. **20–35 min · Retrieve and cite.** Answer your chosen question. Every sentence that states a rule points at a passage: document, page, article, quoted text. Show the passage with the answer and a clickable link to its document location so the officer can inspect the original context.
#### Optional supporting guidance 💡
The ideas below are optional ways to demonstrate the three success criteria in the main challenge brief. They are **not additional criteria or separate scoring items**.
- **Grounding:** use inline citations to exact passages and flag or refuse unsupported claims.
- **Source boundaries:** consider restricting retrieval to the active municipality's configured corpus in the retrieval layer, rather than relying only on a prompt.
- **Legal reasoning:** identify applicable federal, Flemish, provincial and municipal sources and the relevant articles.
- **Officer tasks:** consider explaining steps, drafting replies, summarising policy and retrieving comparable cases where available.
- **Source updates:** let officers add, remove or update sources without engineering help; reflecting changes within minutes is a useful design ambition.
- **Human review:** keep drafts under officer control; never send automatically.
- **Audit and accessibility:** consider logging every answer with its sources and prompt history, and make the interface usable by the least digitally confident officer.
The original brief's ambition to represent **all four legal levels** and **scale across provinces**, not only across municipalities, is supporting guidance for extending the approach, not an additional criterion.

**About the four levels.** Federal, Flemish, provincial and municipal sources each regulate different things, and several can apply to one question at the same time. Do not model them as an automatic ladder in which one level always overrides another. Show which sources you found at each level and how they relate to the question; when they seem to disagree, show both passages and flag it for the officer.

**What this data is.** Real enterprise and establishment records from Digitaal Vlaanderen's public **VKBO** service (*VKBO ondernemingen en vestigingseenheden V3*), which republishes the federal KBO register enriched with addresses from the Flemish address register. Filter: municipality = Schoten. Retrieved on 7 September 2026.

**Known data limitations**
- It is a **partial sample**: the first 1,000 records of a paged response, not the complete register of Schoten, and not a list of 1,000 verified active businesses.
- It **mixes 457 legal entities and 543 establishment units** in one table. Establishments carry their parent enterprise number in `Ondernemingsnr_maatsch_zetel`; legal status is recorded on legal entities only.
- **Import registry identifiers as text.** Spreadsheets may remove their leading zeros if they are treated as numbers.
- **Activity fields are sparse.** The VAT activity code is empty for every row; an RSZ activity code exists for 81 rows.
- **Most parent enterprises are outside the sample.** Only 28 of the 543 establishments have their parent among these 1,000 rows.
- **Some coordinates need validation.** A few points fall well outside Schoten.
- **Placeholder dates.** In the GeoJSON, 1900-01-01 and 9999-12-31 mean "not set" or "open-ended", not real dates.
- **The exact federal KBO snapshot date is not supplied.** The publisher notes a lag of one to three days behind the federal register.
